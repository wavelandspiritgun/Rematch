## 1. One-paragraph abstract

Re.Match is an AI-assisted behavioral-health navigation and recovery-capital platform for people experiencing reentry, substance-use recovery, homelessness, poverty, and mental-health instability. The system converts a structured multidomain intake into a personalized action dossier that matches users to feasible, evidence-aligned services and opportunities across health, housing, recovery support, employment, education, legal navigation, social connection, and purpose-building activities. Re.Match is grounded in SAMHSA’s recovery model, recovery capital theory, CHIME mental-health recovery, trauma-informed care, social determinants of mental health, reentry science, desistance theory, and implementation science. Its core innovation is constraint-aware sequencing: recommendations are filtered by eligibility, documentation, legal restrictions, transportation, cost, location, urgency, readiness, and user goals. The platform is designed as infrastructure rather than therapy, improving access to human services and evidence-based interventions while preserving clinical, legal, and case-management boundaries.

## 2. Problem statement

People navigating reentry, addiction recovery, homelessness, poverty, and mental-health instability often face a dense web of fragmented services. Treatment programs, shelters, legal aid, benefits offices, workforce boards, community colleges, peer supports, and transportation systems may exist, but users must identify which options fit their situation, eligibility, documents, legal status, location, cost, readiness, and urgent needs.

This creates information poverty: a modifiable barrier between existing services and actual access.

## 3. Innovation statement

The innovation is not AI search alone. The innovation is **constraint-aware synthesis**.

Re.Match transforms a user’s life context into structured variables, matches those variables against a constraint-indexed resource database, enriches missing information through live research, and generates a sequenced action dossier with rationale, documents, contacts, timelines, blockers, and fallback options.

## 4. Why now

- Behavioral-health and homelessness systems are under pressure.
- Digital service directories remain fragmented and often unusable for high-need users.
- AI tools can now synthesize complex eligibility, service, and life-domain information, but require strong boundaries and human review.
- Recovery, reentry, and homelessness interventions increasingly recognize that health, housing, employment, community, and purpose are interdependent.

We are requesting cloud/database credits to support a constraint-indexed resource database for Re.Match. The database will store verified service records with eligibility, documentation requirements, location, contact methods, waitlist status where known, cost, target population, reentry/recovery compatibility, source provenance, and freshness metadata. Credits would support a public-interest prototype focused on reentry, recovery, homelessness, and behavioral health.

A pilot could assess feasibility, acceptability, referral accuracy, service-linkage outcomes, recovery capital, mental-health symptoms, housing stability, employment/education progress, and implementation outcomes using RE-AIM, CFIR, EPIS, and Proctor implementation measures.

Re.Match is not designed to diagnose, treat, replace clinicians, replace lawyers, predict criminal risk, or monitor compliance.
