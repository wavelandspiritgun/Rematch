# Re.Match Definitive Project Definition v2.0

**Generated:** 2026-05-07  
**Status:** canonical product/project definition  
**Purpose:** permanent product reference, technical alignment, institutional discussion, prototype planning, public-interest infrastructure definition  
**Not intended as:** a grant-only narrative, marketing slogan, treatment protocol, legal advice system, medical advice system, risk-score engine, or surveillance tool

## 1. Executive definition

**Re.Match** is an AI-assisted opportunity, resource, and action-planning engine for people navigating reentry from incarceration, early recovery from addiction, housing instability, poverty, legal-system involvement, mental-health strain, and adjacent life-disruption conditions.

Its core function is to reduce **information poverty** as a structural barrier to recovery, stability, and reintegration.

A user completes a structured life intake that captures basic needs, housing status, legal constraints, documentation, education, employment, health, mental-health needs, recovery status, skills, interests, values, goals, barriers, support systems, and aspirations. Re.Match converts that intake into a validated profile, matches the profile against a constraint-indexed resource database, enriches missing information through live research, and produces a personalized action dossier.

The output is not a generic list of resources. It is a researched, prioritized, constraint-aware roadmap that explains:

- what opportunities and supports are most relevant;
- why those supports fit the user;
- what requirements, documents, costs, travel barriers, eligibility rules, and waitlists matter;
- what steps should happen first;
- who to contact;
- what to do if the first path fails;
- and how immediate survival moves connect to long-term life construction.

Re.Match is designed to be free at the point of use for the populations it serves.

## 2. Mission

Re.Match exists to help people in crisis, recovery, reentry, poverty, homelessness, and behavioral-health instability identify and pursue a viable path toward a life that is stable, lawful, meaningful, and personally compelling.

The mission is not merely to prevent relapse, recidivism, homelessness, or crisis cycling. The mission is to help people construct a life with enough structure, belonging, health, purpose, opportunity, and self-direction that destructive patterns become less compatible with their emerging identity.

## 3. Core problem: information poverty

People leaving incarceration, entering recovery, escaping homelessness, or surviving poverty are often asked to solve many high-stakes problems at once:

- shelter and long-term housing;
- food, clothing, hygiene, and benefits;
- identification and documents;
- health coverage and medical care;
- mental-health support;
- substance-use treatment and recovery support;
- legal obligations, court requirements, probation/parole conditions, fines, and record relief;
- transportation;
- employment, training, education, and credentials;
- family repair and safe relationship boundaries;
- social isolation;
- digital access;
- and long-term purpose reconstruction.

Traditional referral models often hand users a pamphlet, website, phone number, caseworker suggestion, or undifferentiated service directory. That model assumes the user has time, bandwidth, transportation, executive function, literacy, confidence, stable technology access, emotional regulation, and persistence to turn fragments into action.

For many users, that assumption fails.

Re.Match begins from a different premise:

> The barrier is often not lack of will. The barrier is lack of navigable, personalized, actionable intelligence.

## 4. Founding thesis

A life worth living cannot be built around the avoidance of failure alone.

Fear of relapse, fear of incarceration, fear of homelessness, and fear of consequences may initiate change, but they are insufficient as long-term engines. Sustained recovery and reintegration require a direction worth pursuing. That direction must connect several dimensions:

- what the person is capable of developing;
- what they already care about;
- what gives them meaning;
- what their community genuinely needs;
- and what can eventually sustain them materially.

Re.Match is therefore not only a resource finder. It is a life-path matching system.

## 5. Target population

Re.Match is designed for people experiencing one or more of the following:

- reentry after incarceration;
- probation/parole or other legal-system involvement;
- early recovery from substance use disorder;
- active addiction with interest in stabilization or treatment;
- housing insecurity or homelessness;
- poverty or severe resource instability;
- limited access to technology or reliable research support;
- educational disruption;
- employment barriers;
- medical, mental-health, or documentation barriers;
- social isolation or lack of prosocial support.

The system is especially intended for users who cannot reasonably be expected to conduct exhaustive opportunity research on their own.

## 6. Operating principles

### 6.1 Personalization over generic referral

A resource only matters if it fits the person. Re.Match prioritizes recommendations based on location, eligibility, documents, legal restrictions, transportation access, health needs, recovery preferences, goals, interests, history, strengths, and current capacity.

### 6.2 Actionability over information volume

A long list can become another burden. Re.Match distinguishes the comprehensive candidate resource pool from the personalized action dossier. The dossier should tell the user what to do first, why, and what backup move exists.

### 6.3 Constraints as first-class data

Distance, paperwork, eligibility, waitlists, legal status, mental-health symptoms, literacy, digital access, cost, scheduling, transportation, disability, and stigma are not side notes. They are matching variables.

### 6.4 Purpose alignment

Re.Match does not optimize only for immediate survival. When feasible, it prioritizes opportunities that compound into identity, stability, livelihood, community, and meaning.

### 6.5 Recovery capital growth

The system should increase the user’s practical, social, psychological, institutional, and community resources. Housing, documents, peer support, employment, treatment, benefits, education, transportation, and prosocial community are all recovery-capital assets.

### 6.6 Compounding intelligence

Every high-quality search should improve the system. Newly discovered resources should be deduplicated, normalized, tagged, scored, verified, and added to the database so later users benefit.

### 6.7 Navigation, not professional advice

Re.Match can identify relevant services, clinics, legal aid providers, professional contact points, crisis lines, application steps, and public resources. It does not replace lawyers, doctors, therapists, licensed case managers, crisis responders, or other qualified professionals.

### 6.8 Free at point of use

The primary users generally cannot pay. Funding should come from grants, nonprofit partnerships, public-interest technology programs, cloud/API credits, recovery organizations, reentry organizations, academic partners, philanthropic support, or institutional pilots.

## 7. Scientific product foundation

Re.Match stands on several scientific and practice frameworks:

- SAMHSA recovery model: health, home, purpose, community [S1, S2].
- Recovery capital theory: recovery depends on the resources a person can access and mobilize.
- CHIME mental-health recovery: connectedness, hope, identity, meaning, empowerment [S15].
- Trauma-informed care: safety, peer support, trust, transparency, collaboration, empowerment, voice, and choice [S3].
- Social determinants of mental health: housing, income, legal status, food, transport, digital access, stigma, and social connection are mental-health inputs.
- Addiction neuroscience: addiction affects reward, stress, planning, decision-making, and impulse control systems [S4].
- Reentry science: Risk-Need-Responsivity and Central Eight domains can organize dynamic life barriers when used cautiously and developmentally [S13, S14].
- Desistance and Good Lives Model: reintegration requires identity, agency, prosocial roles, capability building, and meaningful pathways [S16, S29].
- Implementation science: RE-AIM, CFIR, EPIS, Proctor outcomes, and measurement-based care provide evaluation and scale architecture [S21-S27].

## 8. Life-domain model

Re.Match uses a positive-development version of Central Eight-style life domains. The system does not use these domains to label, punish, or predict the user. It uses them to identify what can be built.

| Traditional domain | Re.Match development domain | Example supports |
|---|---|---|
| Antisocial cognition | Cognitive reconstruction, agency, values, decision architecture | CBT, mentorship, reflective planning, education, therapy |
| Antisocial peers | Prosocial network design | peer recovery, gyms, volunteering, classes, clubs, mentorship |
| Family/relationship instability | Relational repair and safe support mapping | counseling, parenting resources, family mediation, boundaries |
| School/work problems | Skill, credential, livelihood pathway | GED, community college, apprenticeships, workforce boards, IPS |
| Substance use | Recovery architecture | MOUD, MAUD, contingency management, peer support, outpatient care |
| Leisure/recreation deficits | Meaningful replacement behavior | sports, art, music, writing, maker spaces, outdoors, volunteering |
| Emotional/personality instability | Regulation, resilience, coping, discipline | therapy, DBT skills, ACT, exercise, mindfulness, coaching |
| Housing/basic needs instability | Stabilization platform | shelter, housing, IDs, benefits, food, phone, transport, coverage |

## 9. Purpose-architecture alignment

Re.Match evaluates long-term opportunity fit through four questions:

1. **Interest:** What does the user care about enough to pursue?
2. **Capacity:** What can the user already do, or plausibly learn?
3. **Community need:** Where would the user’s effort become useful to others?
4. **Livelihood potential:** What can eventually sustain material stability?

When urgent needs dominate, Re.Match prioritizes stabilization. When multiple viable options exist, it prefers pathways with compounding potential.

## 10. System architecture

### 10.1 Intake layer

A structured intake captures life domains, barriers, assets, goals, preferences, constraints, crisis indicators, and user narrative. Intake should support assisted completion through peer workers, caseworkers, community partners, libraries, reentry offices, shelters, clinics, or mobile devices.

### 10.2 Validation and profile layer

The intake is converted into a validated client profile. The profile should be machine-actionable, privacy-conscious, and capable of anonymization or pseudonymization.

### 10.3 Matching layer

The structured profile is matched against a constraint-indexed resource database. Matching should score urgency, feasibility, fit, recovery relevance, mental-health relevance, legal compatibility, transportation feasibility, documentation requirements, and purpose alignment.

### 10.4 Live research layer

When database coverage is incomplete, stale, or insufficient, Re.Match performs live enrichment using official directories, public resource sites, treatment locators, nonprofit directories, workforce data, community-college catalogs, legal-aid directories, government portals, provider pages, and manually verified sources.

### 10.5 Resource normalization layer

Discovered resources are deduplicated, normalized, tagged, scored, and stored with source provenance, eligibility details, constraints, and last-verified dates.

### 10.6 Synthesis layer

The system synthesizes candidate resources into two output classes: a candidate resource pool and a personalized action dossier.

### 10.7 Review layer

High-stakes outputs should be reviewed before use, especially where housing, legal, clinical, crisis, safety, or eligibility implications exist.

### 10.8 Feedback layer

Outcomes, errors, stale records, user progress, blocked referrals, and successful pathways feed back into the system.

## 11. Core outputs

### 11.1 Candidate resource pool

The candidate pool is the comprehensive fallback index. It includes resources that were discovered and evaluated, including options not selected for the top action plan.

Each resource should include:

- name;
- category;
- description;
- location/service radius;
- eligibility;
- documents required;
- costs;
- contact details;
- application steps;
- waitlist status when known;
- transportation notes;
- reentry/recovery compatibility;
- source links;
- last verified date;
- uncertainty notes.

### 11.2 Personalized action dossier

The action dossier is the primary user-facing artifact. It contains:

- user profile summary;
- urgent needs;
- strengths and long-term direction signals;
- top recommendations by domain;
- rationale for each recommendation;
- constraint fit;
- probability-of-success analysis;
- risks and blockers;
- documents needed;
- first action;
- contact instructions;
- timeline and follow-up checkpoints;
- fallback options.

## 12. Recommendation anatomy

Each recommendation should include:

1. Recommendation name.
2. Category/domain.
3. Match rationale.
4. Constraint fit.
5. Probability-of-success analysis.
6. Required documents.
7. Contact pathway.
8. Expected timeline.
9. Risks and mitigations.
10. Verification status.
11. Fallback move.

## 13. What Re.Match is not

Re.Match is not:

- a generic resource directory;
- a vague advice chatbot;
- a replacement for case managers;
- a replacement for therapists, doctors, lawyers, crisis responders, or clinicians;
- a diagnostic tool;
- a court-risk scoring instrument;
- a surveillance product;
- a predictive-policing system;
- a paywalled self-help product;
- or a crisis hotline.

Re.Match is a personalized research, matching, and action-planning engine designed to help people move from instability toward viable life construction.

## 14. Strategic value

### 14.1 For users

Re.Match reduces cognitive load, saves time, identifies better-fitting options, makes next steps visible, and turns overwhelming life complexity into actionable sequence.

### 14.2 For caseworkers and peer workers

Re.Match can reduce repeated manual research, surface targeted resources faster, and create clearer documentation for follow-up.

### 14.3 For organizations

Re.Match can improve referral quality, resource intelligence, program utilization, service-gap visibility, and continuity of support.

### 14.4 For the broader ecosystem

Its database can convert fragmented local knowledge into a public-interest intelligence layer that improves over time.

## 15. Development roadmap

### Phase 1: Tool mastery and architecture validation

Test research, database, scraping, matching, schema, privacy, and synthesis tools.

### Phase 2: Intake and schema refinement

Define the canonical client profile, resource schema, consent language, and missing-field logic.

### Phase 3: Resource database prototype

Build the first constraint-indexed resource database with deduplication, tagging, source provenance, and verification dates.

### Phase 4: Personal prototype

Run the complete pipeline on a founder/live-context case to identify failure modes.

### Phase 5: Closed trusted cohort

Test with a small group, using human review and careful privacy controls.

### Phase 6: Community-partner pilot

Test with one or more reentry, recovery, shelter, library, or behavioral-health partners.

### Phase 7: Public-interest scale

Pursue sustainable funding, evaluation, governance, and broad access while keeping the service free at point of use for target users.

## 16. Canonical operating statement

Re.Match is built on the belief that people are often closer to viable transformation than the system allows them to see.

The missing piece is not always motivation. It is often map quality.

Re.Match gives the user a better map: personalized, researched, constraint-aware, trauma-informed, recovery-oriented, and connected to a life direction worth pursuing.

It does not promise to do the human work for them. It removes unnecessary information burden so the human work can begin.

# Re.Match Scientific Foundation White Paper v1.0

**Generated:** 2026-05-07  
**Status:** scientific foundation and positioning synthesis  
**Purpose:** defensible research-backed explanation of why Re.Match is plausible, useful, measurable, and aligned with evidence-based behavioral-health and reintegration practice

## Executive thesis

Re.Match is best understood as a **behavioral-health navigation, recovery-capital amplification, and reintegration intelligence system**. It does not deliver therapy, diagnosis, legal advice, medical treatment, or criminal-risk prediction. It improves the user’s ability to find, qualify for, sequence, and act on the human services and evidence-based supports already available in the environment.

The intervention target is **information poverty**: the gap between the existence of services and a person’s practical ability to discover, understand, access, and persist through them.

For people experiencing addiction recovery, homelessness, reentry, poverty, and mental-health instability, service access is not a simple information problem. It is a constraint problem. Feasibility depends on documents, eligibility, transportation, legal restrictions, cost, disability, readiness, language, digital access, trauma history, mental-health symptoms, cognitive load, stigma, waitlists, and support. Re.Match treats these as first-class variables.

## 1. Recovery-oriented foundation

SAMHSA defines recovery as a process of change through which people improve health and wellness, live self-directed lives, and strive to reach their full potential [S1]. SAMHSA identifies four major dimensions of recovery: health, home, purpose, and community [S2].

Re.Match maps directly onto this model:

| Recovery dimension | Re.Match translation |
|---|---|
| Health | treatment, MOUD/MAUD routing, mental-health care, primary care, benefits, crisis supports |
| Home | shelter, transitional housing, recovery housing, permanent housing, documents, benefits |
| Purpose | employment, education, credentials, volunteering, creative pursuits, skill development |
| Community | peer support, mentors, support groups, social prescribing, family repair, prosocial belonging |

This means Re.Match is not an abstinence-only directory or a compliance checklist. It is a recovery-oriented navigation system that helps users build the conditions of recovery.

## 2. Recovery capital as the central mechanism

Recovery capital refers to the resources a person can draw on to initiate and sustain recovery. Re.Match operationalizes recovery capital by converting a user’s life context into a plan for increasing practical, social, psychological, institutional, and community supports.

| Recovery-capital domain | Re.Match mechanism |
|---|---|
| Human capital | education, therapy, executive-function scaffolding, treatment access, skills, health coverage |
| Social capital | peers, mentors, support groups, family repair, prosocial networks |
| Physical capital | housing, food, clothing, IDs, phone access, transportation, benefits |
| Community capital | clinics, legal aid, recovery organizations, community colleges, workforce boards, libraries |
| Cultural capital | identity-respecting supports, language access, lived-experience resources, culturally relevant services |
| Purpose capital | meaningful roles, service, work, school, creativity, athletics, community contribution |

The system’s technical function is to locate and sequence these assets.

## 3. CHIME and personal mental-health recovery

The CHIME framework organizes personal recovery around Connectedness, Hope, Identity, Meaning, and Empowerment [S15]. Re.Match uses CHIME not as decoration, but as product architecture.

| CHIME domain | Product mechanism |
|---|---|
| Connectedness | match to peer support, groups, mentors, recovery communities, community activity |
| Hope | show realistic next steps, fallback options, and visible pathways out of paralysis |
| Identity | help users move toward roles such as worker, student, parent, builder, artist, athlete, helper |
| Meaning | match to purpose-aligned education, employment, volunteering, creativity, service |
| Empowerment | preserve choice, explain recommendations, let users select goals and pace |

This is why the dossier must be more than a service list. It must help the user see a plausible future self.

## 4. Trauma-informed design

SAMHSA describes trauma-informed systems as those that realize trauma’s widespread impact, recognize signs of trauma, integrate trauma knowledge into policies and practice, and resist retraumatization [S3]. Re.Match converts this into design requirements:

- minimize unnecessary data collection;
- explain why sensitive questions are asked;
- avoid coercive and shaming language;
- preserve user choice;
- offer fallback options;
- include peer and human supports;
- flag crisis situations for human escalation;
- protect privacy;
- avoid punitive risk-scoring;
- distinguish verified facts from estimates and uncertainty.

## 5. Addiction neuroscience and recovery environment design

NIDA explains that addiction affects reward, stress, decision-making, planning, and impulse-control systems [S4]. Substance-use disorders are also associated with impairments in attention, inhibition, working memory, and decision-making [S19]. Longitudinal alcohol-recovery literature indicates that neuropsychological recovery can vary by function and timeframe [S20].

Therefore, Re.Match should avoid exaggerated claims about a single universal neuroplasticity window. The defensible claim is stronger:

> Early recovery is a high-leverage neurobehavioral transition period in which environment, reinforcement, stress, treatment access, routine, social context, and executive-function scaffolding can influence recovery trajectories.

Re.Match supports recovery indirectly by helping users assemble recovery-supportive conditions:

- medication and treatment access;
- peer support;
- housing stability;
- prosocial routines;
- substance-free rewards;
- employment and education;
- transportation;
- crisis planning;
- meaningful goals.

The system does not claim to directly change the brain. It improves access to the environmental and service conditions under which evidence-based recovery is more likely to occur.

## 6. Evidence-based treatment routing

Re.Match should route users toward evidence-based human services while preserving clinical boundaries.

### MOUD

CDC states that medication treatment for opioid use disorder is associated with reduced overdose risk and overall mortality and identifies buprenorphine, methadone, and naltrexone as FDA-approved medications [S5]. FDA also describes those medications as safe and effective for OUD treatment [S6].

### Co-occurring disorders

SAMHSA defines co-occurring disorders as the coexistence of mental health and substance use disorders [S7]. SAMHSA also states that integrated care is recommended and can improve outcomes such as psychiatric symptoms, substance use, quality of life, housing stability, and arrests [S8].

### Contingency management

SAMHSA’s contingency management advisory describes the evidence base and funding guardrails for using incentives in substance-use treatment [S10]. Re.Match does not deliver contingency management, but can help locate programs that offer it.

### Peer recovery support

SAMHSA describes peer support workers as helping people become and stay engaged in recovery and reduce relapse likelihood [S35]. Re.Match can identify local peer workers, peer-led recovery organizations, mutual-aid groups, and recovery community organizations.

## 7. Reentry, Central Eight, desistance, and Good Lives Model

The Risk-Need-Responsivity model remains influential in correctional rehabilitation and emphasizes matching intensity and services to risk, needs, and responsivity [S13]. However, a 2024 umbrella review found the evidence base for some RNR principles to be mixed and often low quality [S14]. Re.Match should therefore use RNR cautiously as an organizing scaffold, not as a deterministic prediction system.

The project reframes Central Eight-style domains from deficits into development domains. Instead of asking only “what risk should be controlled,” it asks “what life domain can be built.”

The Good Lives Model strengthens this by emphasizing capabilities, strengths, and prosocial routes to valued human outcomes [S16]. Desistance research emphasizes identity, agency, social bonds, stable work, and movement toward valued adult roles [S29].

Grant-safe formulation:

> Re.Match uses reentry science to organize dynamic life domains relevant to reintegration, while maintaining a development-oriented and non-punitive posture.

## 8. Housing and basic needs as behavioral-health infrastructure

HUD’s 2024 AHAR reported more than 770,000 people experiencing homelessness on a single night in January 2024, an 18 percent increase from 2023 [S11, S12].

For Re.Match, housing is not merely a service category. It is a behavioral-health platform. Without a safe place to sleep, preserve medication, maintain hygiene, receive mail, attend appointments, comply with court obligations, and regulate stress, recovery and mental health are structurally undermined.

Re.Match therefore treats basic needs as Tier Zero:

1. safety tonight;
2. food and hygiene;
3. identification/documents;
4. health coverage;
5. phone/internet access;
6. transportation;
7. treatment linkage;
8. peer support;
9. employment/education;
10. long-term purpose path.

## 9. Employment, education, and meaningful role restoration

SAMHSA’s purpose dimension includes meaningful daily activities such as work, school, volunteerism, family caretaking, and creative endeavors [S2]. IPS supported employment meta-analysis shows improved competitive employment outcomes among participants receiving IPS compared with usual services [S17].

Re.Match connects employment and education to mental-health recovery by routing users toward:

- GED/high-school completion;
- community college;
- trade programs;
- apprenticeships;
- reentry-friendly employers;
- workforce boards;
- vocational rehabilitation;
- IPS supported employment;
- paid training pipelines;
- volunteer-to-employment pathways.

## 10. Social prescribing and behavioral activation

Social prescribing links people to voluntary and community-sector activities and services to address social determinants of health and wellbeing [S18]. Behavioral activation approaches target substance-free reward and value-consistent activity in SUD contexts [S34].

Re.Match’s social-prescribing layer should identify:

- gyms and physical training;
- martial arts;
- outdoor groups;
- maker spaces;
- art, music, and writing groups;
- volunteering;
- peer communities;
- faith-neutral community organizations;
- study groups;
- civic service;
- recovery-friendly recreational structures.

This supports the principle that recovery requires replacement rewards, identity, belonging, and activity, not only restraint.

## 11. Cognitive load and executive-function scaffolding

Re.Match’s dossier can function as an executive-function scaffold. It reduces:

- search burden;
- decision burden;
- sequencing burden;
- paperwork burden;
- eligibility burden;
- transportation burden;
- follow-up burden;
- rejection/fallback burden.

For users experiencing executive-function strain, trauma, withdrawal, depression, anxiety, ADHD, unstable sleep, or crisis overload, a structured plan can act as a cognitive prosthetic.

## 12. Implementation-science native architecture

RE-AIM evaluates Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, and Maintenance [S21, S22]. CFIR helps identify real-world implementation barriers and facilitators [S23]. EPIS structures implementation across Exploration, Preparation, Implementation, and Sustainment [S24]. Proctor’s implementation outcomes include acceptability, adoption, appropriateness, feasibility, fidelity, cost, penetration, and sustainability [S25, S26]. Measurement-based care provides a model for repeated outcome monitoring [S27].

Re.Match should be evaluated both as an individual navigation intervention and as organizational infrastructure.

## 13. Scientific positioning statement

Re.Match is a precision navigation layer for people whose recovery and reintegration are blocked by fragmented information, unstable resources, and high cognitive load. It is grounded in recovery science, mental-health recovery, addiction neuroscience, trauma-informed care, social determinants of health, reentry science, housing/employment stabilization, social prescribing, and implementation science. Its innovation is not AI search alone. Its innovation is constraint-aware synthesis: turning a user’s life context into a feasible, evidence-aligned, action-ready roadmap.
