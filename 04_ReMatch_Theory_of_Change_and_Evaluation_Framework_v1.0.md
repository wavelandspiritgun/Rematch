**Generated:** 2026-05-07  
**Purpose:** define Re.Match’s logic model, measurable outcomes, pilot structure, and implementation-science evaluation plan.

## 1. Theory of change

### Core claim

Information poverty is a modifiable barrier to recovery, mental health, housing stability, and reintegration.

### Causal sequence

1. User faces overlapping reentry, recovery, housing, poverty, and mental-health instability.
2. Fragmented service ecosystems create search burden, decision burden, paperwork burden, and follow-up burden.
3. Re.Match turns life context into structured, constraint-indexed data.
4. Matching identifies feasible and evidence-aligned supports.
5. The personalized action dossier reduces cognitive load and sequences action.
6. User is more likely to contact relevant services, complete applications, attend appointments, obtain documents, enter treatment, connect to peers, pursue housing, and begin education/employment steps.
7. Recovery capital, stability, self-efficacy, and prosocial identity increase.
8. Downstream outcomes improve: treatment engagement, housing stability, mental-health functioning, employment/education progress, legal compliance, and reduced crisis cycling.

## 4. Pilot study design

### Title

Pilot Evaluation of Re.Match: AI-Assisted Recovery-Capital Navigation for Adults Experiencing Reentry, Substance-Use Recovery, Housing Instability, and Behavioral-Health Needs.

### Design

Start with a pragmatic mixed-methods feasibility pilot.

### Population

Adults experiencing one or more of:

- recent incarceration or legal-system involvement;
- substance-use recovery or active substance-use disorder;
- homelessness or housing instability;
- poverty/resource instability;
- mental-health treatment need;
- co-occurring SUD and mental-health symptoms.

### Phase 1 sample

30 to 50 users.

### Phase 1 workflow

1. Consent and privacy explanation.
2. Assisted or self-guided intake.
3. Profile validation.
4. Matching and dossier generation.
5. Human review.
6. User delivery.
7. 7-day, 30-day, and 90-day follow-up.

### Primary feasibility endpoints

- intake completion rate;
- dossier generation completion rate;
- human-review correction rate;
- user comprehension;
- user acceptability;
- staff acceptability;
- percent completing at least one action step;
- percent contacting at least one recommended resource;
- percent with at least one successful service linkage.

### Secondary outcomes

- recovery capital change;
- PHQ-9/GAD-7/WHO-5 change;
- treatment linkage;
- peer-support linkage;
- housing applications or placements;
- documents obtained;
- employment or education actions;
- legal-aid or court-support linkage.

### Qualitative interviews

Interview users, peer workers, case managers, reentry staff, behavioral-health staff, housing navigators, and administrators.

Core questions:

- Did Re.Match reduce overwhelm?
- Were recommendations accurate and feasible?
- Did the dossier help action happen faster?
- Did staff discover resources they did not know about?
- What safety/privacy concerns emerged?
- Which fields were missing from the intake?
- Which recommendations failed, and why?

## 5. Implementation-science plan

### RE-AIM

| RE-AIM domain | Re.Match question |
|---|---|
| Reach | Who uses it, and does it reach the intended high-need population? |
| Effectiveness | Does it improve navigation, recovery capital, treatment linkage, housing, and function? |
| Adoption | Which organizations and staff roles adopt it? |
| Implementation | Is it delivered with fidelity, reasonable cost, and acceptable human review? |
| Maintenance | Does it become routine infrastructure and continue improving over time? |

### CFIR

Use CFIR to analyze barriers and facilitators:

- innovation fit;
- relative advantage;
- complexity;
- organizational readiness;
- leadership engagement;
- available resources;
- access to knowledge;
- external partnerships;
- equity and outer-setting pressures.

### EPIS

| EPIS phase | Re.Match activities |
|---|---|
| Exploration | identify partner needs, service gaps, user personas, implementation barriers |
| Preparation | build intake, schemas, review workflows, consent, staff training, data governance |
| Implementation | run pilot, monitor fidelity, track errors, revise dossier model |
| Sustainment | institutionalize updates, train partners, maintain database, monitor equity and cost |

## 6. Data governance for evaluation

- Collect only what is necessary.
- Use informed consent and plain-language explanations.
- Separate identifiable information from research/evaluation data when possible.
- Use human review before high-stakes recommendations.
- Avoid punitive or law-enforcement data sharing.
- Report aggregate outcomes, not stigmatizing individual profiles.
