# Re.Match Ethics, Privacy, and Governance Framework v1.0

**Generated:** 2026-05-07  
**Purpose:** define boundaries, data principles, safety model, anti-surveillance posture, and governance requirements.

## 1. Core ethical position

Re.Match serves people who may be in legally, economically, medically, psychologically, and socially vulnerable positions. The system must therefore be designed with more caution than an ordinary productivity tool.

The ethical center:

> Re.Match exists to increase user agency, not institutional control.

## 2. Boundary statement

Re.Match is a navigational decision-support system. It does not provide:

- diagnosis;
- psychotherapy;
- medical treatment;
- legal advice;
- crisis intervention;
- parole/probation risk scoring;
- law-enforcement surveillance;
- eligibility guarantees;
- benefits determinations;
- court strategy;
- clinical judgment.

It can identify relevant licensed professionals, official services, public resources, application steps, crisis hotlines, legal-aid organizations, treatment providers, and human-support pathways.

## 3. Data minimization

Collect only information needed for matching and action planning. Sensitive fields should be justified by direct utility.

### Required minimization questions

- Does this field improve matching?
- Is it needed for eligibility?
- Is it needed for safety?
- Can it be optional?
- Can it be generalized?
- Can it be deleted after the dossier is generated?
- Can it be stored separately or anonymized?

## 4. Consent and transparency

Users should understand:

- what information is collected;
- why it is collected;
- how it is used;
- whether it is stored;
- who can see it;
- how recommendations are generated;
- where uncertainty exists;
- what Re.Match cannot do.

## 5. Human review model

Human review should be required for:

- crisis or safety flags;
- legal implications;
- clinical/medical implications;
- housing emergencies;
- domestic violence concerns;
- child/family safety concerns;
- high uncertainty recommendations;
- resources with stale or unverifiable information;
- users with complex co-occurring needs.

## 6. Anti-surveillance posture

Re.Match must not become:

- a law-enforcement intelligence tool;
- a probation compliance scoring system;
- a predictive-policing layer;
- a punitive risk score;
- a system for denying aid;
- a behavioral-monitoring tool without consent.

If partners include justice-system organizations, data-sharing boundaries must be explicit. User-benefit data should not become punitive data.

## 7. Trauma-informed product rules

Derived from SAMHSA trauma-informed principles [S3]:

| Principle | Product rule |
|---|---|
| Safety | route crises to humans; avoid exposing sensitive data; use plain explanations |
| Trust/transparency | explain recommendations, uncertainty, and source dates |
| Peer support | include peer recovery and lived-experience resources |
| Collaboration | treat the plan as user-owned |
| Empowerment/voice/choice | offer options and fallback moves, not commands |
| Cultural responsiveness | support identity, language, disability, local context, and lived experience |

## 8. Risk register

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Stale resource data | last-verified dates, source links, user feedback, periodic re-verification |
| Wrong eligibility | confidence labels, human review, direct source citation, fallback options |
| Overclinical claims | navigation-only boundary language |
| Medical/legal harm | route to professionals; avoid advice; include disclaimers and human review |
| Privacy breach | data minimization, encryption, access controls, separation of identifiers |
| Algorithmic bias | equity audit, subgroup monitoring, human review, transparent criteria |
| Digital exclusion | assisted mode, printable dossiers, low-bandwidth interface, library/kiosk workflows |
| User overwhelm | tiered plan: immediate, this week, this month, later |
| Punitive misuse | anti-surveillance agreements and data-sharing restrictions |
| False certainty | uncertainty flags, verification status, rationale transparency |

## 9. Crisis protocol boundaries

When a user may be in immediate danger, Re.Match should not attempt to solve the crisis alone. It should display relevant crisis resources and route to a human responder or emergency service according to local protocol.

The system can include:

- 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline references for U.S. users;
- emergency services direction when immediate danger is indicated;
- local crisis stabilization services where verified;
- domestic violence resources where relevant;
- overdose reversal information and naloxone resources where appropriate.

## 10. Equity and digital access

Re.Match should be designed for users with low digital access. Core requirements:

- mobile-first;
- low-bandwidth mode;
- printable dossier;
- assisted intake;
- plain language;
- multilingual support where possible;
- accessibility for disability;
- phone-call options, not web-only actions;
- partner-site access through libraries, shelters, reentry centers, recovery organizations, and clinics.

## 11. Governance requirements

A mature Re.Match implementation should include:

- privacy policy;
- data retention schedule;
- consent flow;
- human-review standard operating procedures;
- source-verification protocol;
- adverse-event reporting process;
- bias/equity audit;
- partner data-sharing agreements;
- model-output review process;
- resource database maintenance protocol;
- correction request process;
- user deletion/export process.

## 12. Ethical operating statement

Re.Match should give users a better map without taking ownership of their life. The system’s job is to increase agency, reduce avoidable information burden, connect people to human support, and preserve dignity under pressure.
